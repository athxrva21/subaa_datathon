# Appendix A13 — Risk & Compliance, and loss by department

*NovaCorp's own FY2025 Annual Report singles out Risk & Compliance as a material FY2026 risk: 11.8% attrition (above firm average), FAR personal-accountability pressure, and elevated Director-level (L4) departures. Our recommendation leads with Entity_B instead. This page shows we looked, what we found, and why we still prioritised as we did.*

---

## 1. Where the $45.1M actually sits, by department

High-value voluntary exits (Outstanding or High Performer, employee-initiated), priced on the brief's constants:

| Department | Exits | Cost/yr | Roster | **High-value loss rate** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate Operations | 82 | $7.7M | 1,509 | **5.4%** |
| **Risk & Compliance** | 83 | **$8.0M** | 2,022 | **4.1%** |
| Wealth Management | 59 | $5.5M | 1,549 | 3.8% |
| Insurance | 67 | $6.1M | 1,781 | 3.8% |
| Retail Banking | 107 | $9.3M | 3,280 | 3.3% |
| Technology | 96 | $8.0M | 3,032 | 3.2% |
| Executive Leadership | 5 | $0.5M | 230 | 2.2% |

**The Annual Report is right to be concerned.** Risk & Compliance loses high-value people at a materially higher *rate* than the large divisions — Technology carries 50% more headcount and loses the same dollars. On raw dollars R&C looks mid-table; on rate it is second-worst.

**Corporate Operations is worse still, at 5.4%, and appears nowhere in the Annual Report's commentary.** It is flagged there only as "above average" on total attrition (11.6%). On value-weighted loss it is the single worst department in the company. We raise this as a finding requiring follow-up rather than a recommendation, because we have not established a mechanism for it.

---

## 2. Why we did not lead with Risk & Compliance

Three reasons, in order of weight:

**The mechanism is external, and the report already names it.** FAR imposes personal liability on senior regulatory staff, effective March 2024. That makes these roles genuinely harder and intensifies market competition for the people who hold them. This is a **pull** problem in a tight external market — the classic response is compensation benchmarking, which the Annual Report already commits to. We would be recommending what NovaCorp has already decided to do.

**Entity_B's mechanism is internal and therefore actionable.** Entity_B is paid *above* NovaCorp-Origin (0.961 vs 0.937) and leaves anyway; its managers and psychological safety measure fine; what collapsed is trust in senior leadership and sense of purpose, on arrival. Nothing about that requires market conditions to change.

**Entity_B has a proven precedent inside the company.** Entity_A ran the same integration and now sits at 3.6%/yr — below NovaCorp-Origin's own rate. There is no equivalent proof that a compensation response fixes FAR-driven regulatory attrition.

---

## 3. What we would do about R&C

Not a headline recommendation, but not nothing:

- **Apply the redefined regrettable flag here first.** R&C's 83 high-value voluntary exits are exactly the population HR's current flag under-counts. If the definition change is made anywhere first, make it where the loss rate is highest.
- **Separate FAR-driven exits from the rest.** `stated_exit_reason` currently has no category for regulatory-accountability burden, so this cohort is invisible in exit reporting. Adding one costs nothing and makes next year's number diagnosable.
- **Treat Corporate Operations as an open question**, not a conclusion. 5.4% value-weighted loss is worse than the department the Board is watching, and nobody — including us — has established why.

---

## 4. Limitation

Department-level figures here are descriptive splits of the $45.1M, not a separate causal analysis. We have not tested whether R&C's elevated rate is statistically distinguishable from Corporate Operations', and with 82–83 exits per department the confidence intervals overlap substantially. The ranking should be read as indicative, not decisive.
