1 · You are measuring the wrong thing

HR's regrettable_flag fires on 153 departures over two years. Apply a value-based definition — every High Performer or Outstanding employee who chose to leave — and it is 499. Of the 168 High Performers who were managed out, zero were flagged regrettable. Not a low rate. Zero. The flag is applied after the exit by the same function that approved it, so it records whether a departure was convenient, not whether it was costly.

3 · The loss concentrates in one acquisition

Entity_B loses people at 7.0%/yr against Entity_A's 3.6%, and it is the one still running on BambooHR two years after acquisition. It is not a department-mix effect: controlling for department, role level, pay and potential, the gap holds at 1.95× (CI 1.55–2.44).

4 · It is not pay, and not their managers

Six of eight engagement dimensions are fine. Manager effectiveness (p=0.35) and psychological safety (p=0.91) show no difference. Senior leadership trust fell 0.301 and purpose 0.300. Entity_B is paid above NovaCorp-Origin (0.961 vs 0.937 compa-ratio) and leaves anyway. The composite index averaged all eight, so two collapsed scores were diluted by six healthy ones.

5 · You have already solved this once

Entity_A ran the same integration and is now the healthiest cohort in the company — 3.6%/yr, below NovaCorp-Origin's own 4.5%, with survey response recovered to 83.8%. That turns the recommendation from "we hope this helps" into "you have done this before, here, with this workforce". It also sets the success measure.

  1. Redefine what counts as a regrettable loss — include High Performer, separate the flag from the function that approves exits, review quarterly. Restates your baseline immediately.
  2. Finish the Entity_B integration as a trust problem, not a systems problem — stabilise the Senior Manager layer first (9.8%/yr vs 2.9%), then leadership visibility. Not manager training.
  3. Measure acquired cohorts from day one — baseline against company norms at their first survey, and fix the 152 who were never asked.
The data rules out the three most expensive obvious responses: a pay round, a manager training programme, and cutting agency recruitment.

7 · What we recommend against ethics

The obvious move from our own analysis is a flight-risk score built on survey silence. We recommend against deploying it at individual level. It fails the four-fifths rule on age (impact ratio 0.08), role level (0.15) and acquisition cohort (0.08), and its precision is 22.6% — 901 false positives against 263 true ones. The attached dashboard works at cohort level only and contains no employee identifier at all.
What we cannot tell you. We cannot see pre-acquisition Entity_B, so we cannot rule out that they arrived unhappy. Engagement is observed only for responders, so every disengagement figure is a lower bound. Association, not causation. Full limitations on slide 14.
